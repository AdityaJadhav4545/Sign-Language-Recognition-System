Sign language serves as the principal linguistic bridge for over 70 million deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals globally. However, the acute scarcity of fluent signers in mainstream society erects formidable communication barriers across healthcare, education, legal proceedings, and public services. Conventional computer vision solutions for sign language recognition rely heavily on high-dimensional raw video frames and deep 2D/3D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), which introduce prohibitive computational overhead, severe latency bottlenecks, dependency on external GPUs, and extreme vulnerability to varying illumination and background clutter.

This project presents SignSense, a high-efficiency, real-time American Sign Language (ASL) recognition framework capable of classifying 36 static characters (alphabets A–Z and digits 0–9) and dynamic communicative words directly on consumer laptop CPUs. The pipeline employs Google MediaPipe Hands to isolate 21 anatomical 3D skeletal landmarks (63 continuous coordinates) per frame. Coordinates undergo translation-invariant (wrist-relative) and scale-invariant (maximal Euclidean norm) normalization before feeding into StaticGestureNet, a compact 4-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (60,644 parameters). Dynamic gestures are evaluated using DynamicGestureNet, incorporating a 2-layer Bidirectional LSTM with soft temporal attention over a 30-frame window. A stateful PhraseBuilder debounces consecutive frame predictions (12-frame unanimity) and synthesizes sentence whitespace after sustained hand absence (20 empty frames).

Experimental evaluation on a balanced dataset of 10,800 landmark samples achieves a 96.9% validation accuracy, a macro F1-score of 0.970, and an inference latency of under 3 ms on CPU (>30 FPS end-to-end). The system is deployed via an interactive web application powered by Flask, MediaPipe WebAssembly (WASM), and ONNX Runtime Web, supplemented by automated one-click Windows launcher scripts.

Keywords: American Sign Language (ASL), Skeletal Landmarks, MediaPipe, Multi-Layer Perceptron, Bidirectional LSTM, Temporal Attention, ONNX Runtime, Flask, Assistive Computing.

3. INTRODUCTION

3.1 Background & Social Context

Language is the foundational substrate of human civilization, social integration, and cognitive development. While hearing populations communicate primarily through acoustic-oral modalities, deaf and hard-of-hearing communities rely upon sign languages—structured visual-gestural systems equipped with rich phonetic, morphological, syntactic, and semantic grammars. According to epidemiological data published by the World Health Organization (WHO), more than 430 million people worldwide require disabling hearing loss rehabilitation, a figure anticipated to exceed 700 million by 2050.

Among the world's established sign systems, American Sign Language (ASL) represents one of the most thoroughly standardized, comprising both manual fingerspelling (alphabets A–Z and numbers 0–9 used to spell names and technical terms) and lexical signs representing complete concepts, verbs, and phrases. However, fewer than 0.1% of the non-deaf global population possesses functional signing literacy. As a consequence, deaf individuals encounter persistent systemic friction during essential social encounters, university lectures, medical examinations, and public transit navigation.

3.2 Motivation

While certified human sign language interpreters provide invaluable services, they are constrained by prohibitive financial costs (typically $70–$150/hour), severe regional shortages, and scheduling delays. This scarcity has fueled demand for automated, real-time Machine Learning-driven Sign Language Recognition (SLR) systems.

Historically, automated SLR research has been hampered by two opposing technological extremes:

  • Sensory Data Gloves: Early engineering approaches utilized cyber-gloves equipped with flex sensors, tactile resistors, and inertial measurement units (IMUs). While accurate, these physical devices are ergonomically cumbersome, fragile, expensive, and require continuous sensor recalibration.
  • Raw Pixel Deep Learning (2D/3D CNNs): Subsequent vision systems processed raw camera streams using heavy convolutional architectures (e.g., VGG-16, ResNet-50, I3D). However, raw pixel inputs introduce massive redundant data ($1920 \times 1080 \times 3$ values per frame), demand dedicated NVIDIA GPUs, and overfit heavily to background lighting, clothing, shadows, and ethnic skin-tone variations.

These limitations underscored the imperative to develop SignSense: an intelligent, camera-agnostic, vision-based system that operates entirely on geometric skeletal hand coordinates, providing invariant, real-time sign recognition on standard consumer hardware.

3.3 Problem Statement

The goal of this academic capstone project is to architect, train, optimize, and deploy an end-to-end American Sign Language recognition system that:

  1. Performs instantaneous, highly accurate classification across 36 static ASL characters (A–Z, 0–9) and dynamic communicative word sequences.
  2. Executes with sub-30ms latency on commodity laptop CPUs at 30 FPS without requiring discrete GPU accelerators.
  3. Applies mathematical coordinate normalization ensuring complete invariance to hand size, user-to-camera distance, and planar translation.
  4. Eliminates prediction jitter through stateful temporal debouncing while automatically assembling fluent sentences with whitespace insertion.
  5. Provides an accessible, full-stack browser-based deployment utilizing WebAssembly and ONNX Runtime Web.

Synthesis and Architectural Gap

Prior research demonstrates that while raw pixel CNNs achieve reasonable laboratory accuracy, they fail in consumer deployment due to hardware cost and environmental brittleness. By contrasting raw image representations with geometric landmark representations, our proposed architecture utilizes MediaPipe as an abstraction layer, transforming raw video into low-dimensional normalized coordinate vectors. This reduces model complexity by orders of magnitude while accelerating inference to sub-3ms CPU execution.

7.2 Mathematical Normalization Formulation

Given 21 raw landmarks $\mathbf{P} = \{\mathbf{p}_0, \mathbf{p}_1, \dots, \mathbf{p}_{20}\}$ with $\mathbf{p}_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i)$, where $\mathbf{p}_0$ represents the wrist:

1. Translation Invariance (Wrist to Origin):

$$\mathbf{p}'_i = \mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_0, \quad \forall i \in \{0, \dots, 20\}$$

2. Scale Invariance (Euclidean Norm Scaling):

$$s = \max_{j \in \{0, \dots, 20\}} \sqrt{x'_j{}^2 + y'_j{}^2 + z'_j{}^2} + 10^{-6}, \quad \mathbf{p}''_i = \frac{\mathbf{p}'_i}{s}$$

3. Feature Flattening:

$$\mathbf{x} = [x''_0, y''_0, z''_0, x''_1, y''_1, z''_1, \dots, x''_{20}, y''_{20}, z''_{20}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{63}$$

8. METHODOLOGY AND IMPLEMENTATION

8.1 StaticGestureNet Architecture

The static recognition neural network is formulated as an optimized Multi-Layer Perceptron engineered in PyTorch:

  • Layer 1: $\mathbf{h}_1 = \text{Dropout}_{0.3}(\text{ReLU}(\text{BatchNorm}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}_1)))$, with $\mathbf{W}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times 63}$
  • Layer 2: $\mathbf{h}_2 = \text{Dropout}_{0.3}(\text{ReLU}(\text{BatchNorm}(\mathbf{W}_2 \mathbf{h}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2)))$, with $\mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{128 \times 256}$
  • Layer 3: $\mathbf{h}_3 = \text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_3 \mathbf{h}_2 + \mathbf{b}_3)$, with $\mathbf{W}_3 \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 128}$
  • Classification Output: $\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{W}_4 \mathbf{h}_3 + \mathbf{b}_4$, with $\mathbf{W}_4 \in \mathbb{R}^{36 \times 64}$
  • Total Trainable Parameters: 60,644 weights and biases initialized via Kaiming normal distribution.

8.2 DynamicGestureNet with Temporal Attention

For sequential dynamic signs, a 30-frame sequence $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{30 \times 63}$ is processed by a 2-layer Bidirectional LSTM. A learned attention vector computes time-step importance weights:

$$\alpha_t = \frac{\exp(\mathbf{w}_a^T \tanh(\mathbf{W}_a \mathbf{h}_t + \mathbf{b}_a))}{\sum_{k=1}^{30} \exp(\mathbf{w}_a^T \tanh(\mathbf{W}_a \mathbf{h}_k + \mathbf{b}_a))}, \quad \mathbf{c} = \sum_{t=1}^{30} \alpha_t \mathbf{h}_t$$

8.3 Key Implementation Logic

# Static MLP Architecture Definition (src/models/static_mlp.py)
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class StaticGestureNet(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, num_classes: int = 36, dropout: float = 0.3):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(63, 256),
            nn.BatchNorm1d(256),
            nn.ReLU(inplace=True),
            nn.Dropout(p=dropout),
            nn.Linear(256, 128),
            nn.BatchNorm1d(128),
            nn.ReLU(inplace=True),
            nn.Dropout(p=dropout),
            nn.Linear(128, 64),
            nn.ReLU(inplace=True),
            nn.Linear(64, num_classes)
        )
    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        return self.net(x)
# Temporal Anti-Jitter Debounce Engine (src/phrase_builder.py)
def push(self, sign: str | None):
    if sign is None:
        self._empty_count += 1
        self._buf.clear()
        if self._empty_count >= self.space_frames and not self._space_inserted and self._tokens:
            if self._tokens[-1] != " ":
                self._tokens.append(" ")
                self._space_inserted = True
    else:
        self._empty_count = 0
        self._space_inserted = False
        self._buf.append(sign)
        # Commit sign only after 12 consecutive identical predictions
        if len(self._buf) == self.hold_frames and len(set(self._buf)) == 1:
            self._tokens.append(sign)
            self._buf.clear()

11. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE

11.1 Conclusion

The Sign Language Recognition System (SignSense) demonstrates an effective, production-grade assistive communication platform for real-time American Sign Language recognition. By decoupling high-dimensional visual perception from gestural classification through Google MediaPipe Hands, the system eliminates traditional GPU hardware prerequisites, achieving 96.9% validation accuracy across 36 static classes with sub-3ms inference latency on commodity laptop CPUs.

The stateful temporal debouncing algorithm suppresses per-frame flicker, while automatic whitespace synthesis enables natural sentence creation. The deployment architecture, featuring in-browser WebAssembly execution and one-click Windows launchers, delivers an accessible, robust tool that facilitates smoother communication between signing and non-signing communities.

11.2 Future Scope

  1. MediaPipe Holistic Integration: Transitioning to MediaPipe Holistic to track both hands simultaneously alongside facial expressions and torso pose, enabling complex bimanual signing in British Sign Language (BSL) and Indian Sign Language (ISL).
  2. Lightweight Language Model Autocorrection: Incorporating an n-gram or quantized GPT-2 model into the `PhraseBuilder` to autocorrect fingerspelled strings into grammatically fluent sentences.
  3. Speech Synthesis Voice Feedback: Integrating the Web Speech Synthesis API to vocalize assembled sentences aloud, facilitating reciprocal spoken conversations for non-signing listeners.
  4. Mobile Edge Portability: Exporting quantized ONNX models to TensorFlow Lite (TFLite) and Apple CoreML for native offline mobile applications on Android and iOS devices.
